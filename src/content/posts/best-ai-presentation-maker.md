## Verdict at a Glance

**The short answer:** Gamma is the fastest free start, Plus AI is the safest if the deck must stay a real PowerPoint file, and if you already pay for Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, use the AI already in your plan.

- **Best overall (for link-shared decks):** Gamma — fastest from prompt to a designed-looking deck, with a real free plan.
- **Best for real PowerPoint files:** Plus AI — works inside PowerPoint and Google Slides, so there is no export step.
- **Best free plan for design work:** Canva — a permanent free tier with PowerPoint export, not a trial.
- **Best for client decks:** Beautiful.ai — Smart Slides keep every slide on-brand, but there is no free plan.
- **Best if you already pay for Microsoft 365:** Copilot in PowerPoint — included in Personal and Family plans.
- **Best if you already pay for Google:** Gemini in Google Slides — builds editable decks inside Slides.
- **Best for researched decks:** GenSpark AI Slides — live web research with sources; PowerPoint export needs Plus.
- **Best for document-to-deck exports:** SlideSpeak — cleanest PowerPoint exports.

## Two things the top-5 lists skip

Most roundups rank these tools by how pretty the first draft looks. That is the wrong test: the first draft is never the deck you present. [Zapier's 2026 roundup](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-presentation-maker/) tested 40-plus apps for a month, but it judges the tools mainly inside their own editors. Two questions matter more.

**Pain point 1: does the PowerPoint export actually survive?** A deck that looks perfect in a web editor can come back with broken layouts or charts flattened into images. [24Slides ran the same prompt through 10 tools](https://24slides.com/presentbetter/best-ai-presentation-tools) and found exactly that: Gamma's layouts "often break" on export, and Canva "flattens charts to images."

[StoryChief opened every export on desktop](https://storychief.io/blog/best-ai-presentation-maker) before scoring. Gamma wins for link-shared decks, while Plus AI wins when teammates need editable text boxes. If someone expects a .pptx file, test the export in desktop PowerPoint first.

**Pain point 2: what does it cost once the free credits run out?** Almost every tool here paywalls editable PowerPoint export, as 24Slides' cost breakdown shows, and free credits are usually a one-time grant. Gamma's 400 credits never refresh, Beautiful.ai has no free plan, and Plus AI's free entry is a 7-day trial that needs a card.

The honest comparison is the yearly cost of the plan that lets you export and keep working: roughly $100–$296 a year for one person on annual billing, or $120–$540 if you pay month to month.

## Gamma: the fastest free start

Gamma is the quickest route from a prompt to a designed-looking deck. Since Gamma 5 launched in October 2026, creation starts with a conversation about your goal and audience, according to [dev.to's November 2026 comparison](https://dev.to/neuraitool/best-ai-presentation-makers-in-2026-6-tools-compared-338k).

The free plan is real but finite: 400 AI credits at signup, which do not refresh. That covers 5–10 decks, with a "Made with Gamma" badge on exports.

Paid plans start at $9–$12 per seat a month by billing cycle (Plus, $108–$144 a year). Pro runs $18–$25 a month ($216–$300 a year) and adds [premium image models](/posts/best-ai-image-generators/), custom branding and detailed analytics. The API itself is open on every plan, billed in credits, per [Gamma's pricing page](https://gamma.app/pricing).

The catch is the export problem above: Gamma builds fluid web "cards," not fixed slides, and 24Slides found its PowerPoint exports frequently break formatting.

If you present from a Gamma link, that does not matter. If you must hand over a .pptx, budget time to fix layouts, or pick Plus AI. Check the numbers the AI writes, too: a prompted deck can invent statistics.

## Canva: the best free plan for design work

Canva is not a presentation-only tool, and that is exactly why it wins for many people, as dev.to's comparison also notes. Magic Design builds a complete deck from a prompt, and the same account then makes the social posts and handouts. If slides are one small part of a wider design workload, Canva Pro at $144–$216 a year is the best value here.

The free plan is permanent, not a trial: templates, stock assets, collaboration and 5GB of storage, with PowerPoint and PDF export included.

The AI allowance is the limit. The free plan gets up to 20 Standard or Premium AI uses a month from one shared pool, per [Canva's pricing page](https://www.canva.com/pricing) and [AI Worth It's November check](https://www.aiworthit.com/blog/canva-ai-review/). Pro costs $12–$18 a month by billing cycle ($144–$216 a year) and adds the full Magic Studio suite, Brand Kit and background remover.

The same export warning applies: 24Slides found Canva flattens charts to images on PowerPoint export, so a data-heavy deck loses its editable charts. For a marketing deck that is a fair trade; for a board deck built on numbers, it is not. See also our roundup of the [best free AI image upscaler](/posts/best-free-ai-image-upscaler/).

## Beautiful.ai: the best for client-facing decks

Beautiful.ai takes the opposite approach to Gamma. Its Smart Slides auto-arrange every slide as you type, which keeps layouts clean for non-designers. There are 300-plus Smart Slide layouts, and the AI can build a full deck from a prompt, outline or source material. For consultants sending decks to clients weekly, that guardrail is the whole product. A clean headshot on the title slide helps, too; see our roundup of the [best AI headshot generator](/posts/best-ai-headshot-generator/).

There is no permanent free plan. Beautiful.ai says so plainly on its own blog, "[Is Beautiful.ai Free to Use?](https://mktg.beautiful.ai/blog/is-beautiful-ai-free-to-use)".

The only way in is a 14-day trial that requires a credit card and auto-charges if you do not cancel. Pro costs $14.50–$45 a month depending on billing cycle ($174–$540 a year), and Team is $40–$50 per user a month. Students with a verified .edu email get a year free.

Weekly decks make Pro's annual billing reasonable; three decks a year, Gamma's free tier is the answer. AI Insiders' [Beautiful.ai review](https://www.aiinsiders.net/tools/reviews/beautiful-ai) found the same: nearly half of its 250-plus Trustpilot reviews are one star, mostly over trial billing and cancellation, not the slides. Set a reminder for day 13 of the trial.

## Plus AI: the safest for real PowerPoint files

Plus AI is the specialist pick, and the one this article recommends most strongly for office workers. It is an add-on inside both Google Slides and PowerPoint, generating and editing slides without forcing you into a new app.

It writes native shapes and text boxes into the file you already own, so there is no export step and nothing to break. LitmusTools' [Plus AI review](https://litmustools.com/review/plus-ai/) puts it plainly: "your slides stay in Google Slides or PowerPoint as native, editable files, with no export tax."

Pricing is per user by billing cycle: Basic $10–$15 a month (1,500 credits), Pro $20–$25 (3,000 credits, document uploads and AI images), Team $30–$40 (custom branding and shared templates), per [Plus AI's pricing page](https://plusai.com/pricing) and [The Rundown's pricing check](https://www.therundown.ai/tools/plus-ai-powerpoint-maker). Every paid plan starts with a 7-day free trial with 1,000 credits, and the trial requires a credit card, as 24Slides flags. There is no permanent free plan.

The trade-off is design: decks look like your template, not like Gamma's. That is a feature, not a flaw, if your company already has a slide master. Our comparison of the [best AI meeting assistant for small teams](/posts/best-ai-meeting-assistant-small-teams/) covers the same logic.

## Copilot in PowerPoint: best if you already pay for Microsoft 365

If your company already runs on Microsoft 365, Copilot in PowerPoint is the path of least resistance, as dev.to's comparison also notes. It drafts a presentation from a prompt, turns a Word document into slides and rewrites existing slides, inside the file you already have open.

For individuals, Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium run $9.99–$19.99 a month ($99.99–$199.99 a year), per [Microsoft's pricing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365) and Heydecks' [PowerPoint AI comparison](https://heydecks.com/blog/best-ai-for-powerpoint). Business users need the Copilot add-on, $18–$21 per user a month on annual billing, per [Microsoft's Copilot business pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/business). There is no standalone free plan, so Copilot only makes sense if you already pay for Microsoft 365.

## Gemini in Google Slides: best if you already pay for Google

Gemini in Google Slides plays the same role as Copilot, but for Google's ecosystem. It generates new slides from a prompt, shows you an outline first, and references Drive files.

Access needs a paid Google plan. Google AI plans run $4.99–$199.99 a month, and Pro, the mid-tier plan, also bundles 5TB of storage and higher Gemini limits, per [Google's AI plans page](https://gemini.google/subscriptions/) and [The 64th's comparison](https://the64th.com/gemini-vs-microsoft-copilot/).

The cheaper Plus tier does not include the Slides deck builder. It rolled out on Pro, Ultra and Workspace Business Standard and above, per [Google's Workspace help page](https://support.google.com/a/answer/15400543) and Heydecks. If your files live in Drive, Gemini is the natural choice: the deck is an editable Slides file from the start.

## GenSpark and SlideSpeak: the researched-deck specialists

Two newer tools cover one job: turning research or a document into a deck.

**GenSpark AI Slides** was 24Slides' top overall pick, scoring 9.2 out of 10: it scans the live web, pulls fresh data with sources, and builds fixed 16:9 slides in under two minutes. The free plan gives 100 credits a day, but PowerPoint export is paywalled behind Plus, $19.99–$24.99 a month depending on billing cycle ($240–$300 a year), per [GenSpark's membership plans](https://www.genspark.ai/helpcenter/membership-plans) and [Activepieces' pricing check](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/genspark-pricing-2026-free-vs-plus-vs-pro-plans). SortWell's [GenSpark review](https://sortwell.net/en/ai-tools/genspark-slides-review) warns even the paid export "still needs a quick alignment pass in PowerPoint."

**SlideSpeak** turns documents into presentations and scored 8.7 out of 10 in 24Slides' test for the cleanest PowerPoint exports: editable vectors, not flattened images. The free plan gives 100 credits at sign-up, roughly three decks, with no file export, per [SlideSpeak's pricing page](https://slidespeak.co/pricing) and [its credits guide](https://slidespeak.co/help/articles/how-credits-work-in-slidespeak). Paid plans start at $24.65–$29 a month by billing cycle, the highest entry price here, confirmed by [GenPPT's October pricing check](https://genppt.com/blog/best-slidespeak-alternatives).


## How to choose in five minutes

1. **Check what you already pay for.** Microsoft 365? Use Copilot. Google AI Pro or Workspace? Use Gemini in Slides.
2. **Check where the deck must end up.** If someone else will edit the .pptx, pick Plus AI. If a web link is fine, Gamma is faster.
3. **Check how often you present.** One deck a month or less: Gamma's free credits or Canva Free. Weekly client decks: Beautiful.ai Pro or Gamma Plus.
4. **Check the export before you pay.** Generate one real deck, open the .pptx in desktop PowerPoint, and check for broken layouts and flattened charts.
5. **Check the numbers.** AI decks invent statistics, so verify every figure against its source.



## FAQ

**What is the best AI presentation maker overall?**

Gamma: the fastest from prompt to a designed-looking deck, with a usable free plan. If the deck must stay a real PowerPoint file, Plus AI is the better pick.

**Is there a truly free AI presentation maker?**

Gamma and Canva both have permanent free plans: Gamma gives 400 one-time credits, Canva Free includes limited AI uses a month plus PowerPoint export. Beautiful.ai and Plus AI have no free plan, only card-required trials.

**Can AI presentation makers export to PowerPoint?**

Yes, but quality varies. Plus AI, Copilot and Gemini never export: they work inside PowerPoint or Slides, so the file is native. Gamma and Canva export to .pptx, but same-prompt tests found broken layouts and flattened charts. Open the file in desktop PowerPoint first.

**Do I need to pay if I already have Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace?**

Probably not: Copilot is included in Microsoft 365 Personal and Family, and Gemini in Slides comes with Google AI Pro or eligible Workspace plans. Try the AI you already own first.


## The bottom line

Start with Gamma for the fastest free deck, or Canva if slides are part of wider design work. Move to Plus AI when the deck must stay a native PowerPoint file, to Beautiful.ai for client decks, and to Copilot or Gemini when you already pay for Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. Judge any tool on the exported file and the yearly cost, not the first draft.
