## What One Finished Image Really Costs

Most comparison guides list a starting monthly price and stop there. That number tells a seller almost nothing: product photography is priced in credits, exports, or images per month, and the three units are not the same thing.

A $9–$19 plan with 30–200 images costs about $0.10–$0.30 per image; a $7.50–$12.99 plan with 1,000 exports costs about a cent per export. The cheaper sticker price can be the more expensive tool for a 200-SKU store.

Re-rolls widen the gap: a scene rarely lands first try, and generation-metered tools charge for every retry, while export-metered tools charge only when an image is saved.

Before subscribing, count the finished images a month needs, budget for two rejected generations per keeper, and divide the monthly price by the realistic number of keepers. Unused allowance does not roll over on most tools here.

A seller listing 40 products with six images each needs 240 finished images a month. That is already over Pebblely Basic's 200-image allowance before a single re-roll, but comfortable inside Photoroom Pro's 1,000 exports.

Upscaling costs extra: a 2K or 4K export can consume several times the credits of a standard one. Our guide to the [best free AI image upscaler](/posts/best-free-ai-image-upscaler/) covers standalone options when built-in upscaling runs short.

## Can You Legally Sell With These Images?

The second gap is commercial rights: most guides mention "commercial use" in one line and move on, but the details decide whether a listing is safe.

**Who owns the output?** Pebblely's FAQ states the seller owns the images generated; Claid.ai's pricing FAQ states its images carry no Claid copyright; getimg.ai includes commercial rights on every paid plan, according to its own product blog.

Photoroom's help center states a paid subscription covers commercial use of its templates and AI-generated content, including e-commerce listings, and its terms state the customer owns the content, though sellers must still check AI output for protected material. When a vendor does not state ownership clearly, treat the output as unusable for paid listings.

**Does the free plan allow commercial use?** Photoroom's free plan is limited to personal, non-commercial use, according to its own help center. A store built on it breaks the terms from the first listing.

Flair.ai's terms add a different trap: images from the Free, Pro and Pro+ plans may appear in a public community gallery, and only Scale is private by default, according to Flair's own FAQ and terms of service. On Flair, Pro covers commercial use for an individual creator, while Pro+ covers teams of two or more.

**Is there any indemnification?** Adobe Firefly offers IP indemnification for eligible outputs on qualifying agreements, according to Adobe. The cover follows the agreement signed, not every paid plan. No other tool here offers an equivalent promise. For regulated categories such as beauty or supplements, that protection outweighs a prettier render.

Marketplace rules add a final layer. Amazon's main image must sit on a pure white background with no text, logos, or badges; lifestyle scenes belong in secondary slots.

A scene that changes the product's color, label, or shape is a misrepresentation that starts returns, so inspect every image at full zoom before it goes live. For listing copy, pair the images with one of the [best AI copywriting tools for ecommerce](/posts/best-ai-copywriting-tools-ecommerce/).

This summarizes vendor terms as of November 2026 and is not legal advice.

## The Six Tools, Reviewed

### 1. Photoroom — Best Overall for Most Sellers

Photoroom is a mobile-first editor built around one idea: the uploaded product stays the product. Background removal, studio backgrounds, AI scenes, and AI fashion models all start from a real photo, so it holds SKU fidelity better than tools that rebuild the item from a prompt.

Batch editing handles up to 250 images at once, and Max ($20.99–$34.99 a month) adds a Shopify integration.

Photoroom's pricing page lists paid plans at $7.50–$99 a month depending on tier and billing: Pro includes 8,000 credits and 1,000 exports; Max includes 25,000 credits and 3,000 exports.

The two pools are separate. The credits pay for AI generations such as AI backgrounds, model shots and video, while the export cap counts every image saved, including batch exports and Shopify syncs. The API is billed separately, from two cents per image.

**Pros:** fastest path from phone photo to listing; export metering keeps the per-image cost low. **Cons:** scenes are compositing and can look pasted; Trustpilot scores it 2.2 out of 5 from 279 reviews, citing sudden plan changes and slow support.

That score reflects support and plan-change complaints, not the editing workflow itself. Photoroom is still the fastest path from a phone photo to a listing, and it holds SKU fidelity better than prompt-rebuilt tools. Pay monthly until the workflow has earned trust.

### 2. Pebblely — Best for Low-Cost Lifestyle Scenes

Pebblely does one job: put a packshot into a new background. Upload a product photo, pick from 40+ themes or write a custom prompt, and the tool isolates the product and builds the scene with matching shadows and reflections. No prompt engineering is needed.

Pebblely's pricing page lists plans at $9–$39 a month: Lite covers 30 images, Basic 200 with bulk generation, and Pro 500. Unused images do not roll over, and the FAQ states no refunds are offered.

Annual billing drops the effective monthly rate to about $7–$32 a month. Basic works out to about $14–$15 a month and Pro to about $31–$32 a month on annual billing, with Lite around $7–$8 a month per third-party pricing checks.

**Pros:** lowest per-generation price here at about $0.08–$0.10 per raw generation on Basic and Pro. **Cons:** no public API or working Shopify sync is listed as of late 2026. Third-party audits note the old API docs and Shopify listing disappeared after Pebblely's March 2026 rebuild; simple scenes can look templated.

### 3. Flair.ai — Best for Art-Directed Brand Sets

Flair.ai is a drag-and-drop staging canvas for brands that already know how their products should look. Custom models learn a product or a human model, templates keep campaign sets consistent, and the tool adds on-model fashion shots, ads, and short videos.

It is the only tool here where the workflow starts with a style bible, not a single photo.

Flair's pricing page lists plans at $0–$55 a month: Free includes 5 generated images; Pro costs $8–$10 a month depending on billing; Pro+ costs $26–$35 a month for 80 images; Scale Tier 1 costs $38–$55 a month for 150 images.

Pro+ and Scale include a company commercial license; instant generation consumes four times the standard quota. Flair's pricing page states no monthly image allowance for Pro, so its per-image cost cannot be calculated from the plan page alone.

**Pros:** custom models keep a brand world consistent; the free tier lets a seller try real generations. **Cons:** Pro+ works out to the highest per-image cost here, about forty-four cents; lower tiers publish outputs to a public gallery.

### 4. Claid.ai — Best for Large Catalogs and API Workflows

Claid.ai is the operations pick: instead of scenes, it sells a credit pool. Background removal costs 1 credit, an AI Photoshoot 4 credits at standard quality or 10 at Studio Quality, and upscaling 2–8 credits, according to Claid's pricing page.

On Essentials ($9–$15 a month for 500 credits), that works out to about $0.07–$0.30 per generation before re-rolls: a Standard AI Photoshoot costs 4 credits, about $0.07–$0.12, and Studio Quality costs 10 credits, about $0.18–$0.30. Pro adds batch processing, a priority queue, and brand kits.

Claid's pricing page lists plans at $9–$49 a month: the free trial includes 50 credits; Essentials costs $9–$15 a month depending on billing, for 500 credits at up to 2K; Pro costs $35–$49 a month for 2,000 credits with 2K/4K generation. API credits are sold separately in blocks of 1,000.

**Pros:** a transparent per-operation credit menu makes the true cost calculable in advance; API access suits automated pipelines. **Cons:** scene invention is not the main value; credit math punishes sellers who do not plan operations.

### 5. Adobe Firefly — Best for IP Safety and Photoshop Teams

Firefly is the safe choice for brands with legal review. Its models are trained on licensed Adobe Stock content, public-domain material, and openly licensed material, according to Adobe, and its IP indemnification on qualifying agreements is the strongest commercial protection here.

The practical reason to buy it is Photoshop: Generative Fill and Generative Expand fix backgrounds inside the file a retoucher would open anyway.

Adobe's plans page lists plans at $9.99–$199.99 a month: Standard includes 2,000 generative credits; Pro 4,000; Pro Plus 10,000; Premium 50,000. Standard image generations are unlimited on paid plans; credits pay for premium features and partner models, and reset monthly without rollover.

**Pros:** documented IP coverage and commercially trained models; deep Photoshop workflow for retouching. **Cons:** Firefly's own model lags the newest dedicated models on photorealism; no persistent product-reference system. Teams comparing video options can weigh Firefly against our [Higgsfield vs Runway vs Kling](/posts/higgsfield-vs-runway-vs-kling/) comparison.

### 6. getimg.ai — Best Multi-Model Studio in One Account

Getimg.ai is a general creative platform with a product-photography edge: 25+ image models including FLUX, GPT Image, and Nano Banana, plus an Elements system that stores a product reference and reuses it across every generation.

Create a Product Element once, and later generations keep the same product instead of drifting. It is the closest thing here to a catalog library.

getimg.ai's pricing page lists plans at $15–$199 a month: Entry includes 8,000 credits, Core 19,000, Plus 42,500, and Ultra 107,500. There is no free plan, and commercial rights are included on all paid plans, according to the vendor's own blog.

**Pros:** widest model choice in one subscription; persistent product references solve the consistency problem that breaks most generators. **Cons:** credit costs vary by model, so the per-image price is hard to predict; no free tier means the cheapest experiment costs a full month.

## How to Choose in Ten Minutes

Start from the listing: the channel decides the spec, the spec decides the asset, and the asset decides the tool.

- **Under 50 SKUs, phone photos, listings this week:** Photoroom Pro. Export metering and batch editing cover the workload; the free plan cannot be used commercially, so pay from day one.
- **Lifestyle scenes on a tight budget:** Pebblely Basic. At about $0.08–$0.10 per raw generation (roughly $0.25–$0.30 per keeper after two re-rolls), it is the cheapest scene generator here if you publish under ~65 finished scenes a month.
- **A brand look across campaigns:** Flair.ai Pro+. Custom models keep the world consistent; note the public-gallery default on lower tiers.
- **Hundreds of SKUs or an automated pipeline:** Claid.ai Pro, plus its API. Calculate the credit cost per operation first.
- **Legal review or regulated products:** Adobe Firefly Standard, and confirm the indemnification terms cover the planned use.
- **One account for images, edits, and video:** getimg.ai Core. The Elements system is the deciding feature for teams reusing the same products.

## FAQ

**Can I use AI-generated product photos on Amazon?**
Yes, with conditions. Amazon requires the main image on a pure white background with no text or badges, showing the actual product. Lifestyle scenes belong in secondary slots.

An image that changes the product's shape, label, or color risks a misrepresentation complaint and returns. Inspect every image at full zoom against the physical item.

**Which AI image generator is cheapest per product photo?**
On headline allowances, Photoroom Pro works out to about a cent per export and Pebblely Basic to about ten cents per raw generation. Re-rolls change the math: budget for two rejected generations per keeper, and divide the monthly price by the images actually published.

Unused credits do not roll over on most tools here.

**Do I own the images these tools generate?**
It depends on the vendor: Pebblely's FAQ states the seller owns generated images, Claid.ai states its images carry no Claid copyright, and getimg.ai includes commercial rights on paid plans.

Photoroom allows commercial use on paid plans but forbids it on the free plan, and Flair.ai publishes lower-tier outputs to a public gallery by default. Check the vendor's terms before publishing.

**Is a free plan enough for a small store?**
Usually not. Photoroom's free plan bans commercial use, Flair.ai's free tier allows only 5 images and publishes them publicly, and Claid.ai's free trial is 50 one-time credits.
