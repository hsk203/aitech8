## Consent First: What Each Tool Asks Before It Clones a Voice

Most roundups treat consent as a footnote. It should be the first filter, because cloning a voice without permission is the fastest way to get an account banned — and in some places, to break the law.

Consumer Reports audited six voice-cloning products in March 2025: ElevenLabs, Speechify, PlayHT and Lovo only asked users to tick a box saying they had the right to clone the voice, while Descript required the speaker to record a consent statement and Resemble AI required a real-time recording for the first clone, according to [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2025/03/consumer-reports-assessment-of-ai-voice-cloning-products/). That audit is history now: Resemble AI stopped selling voice cloning in August 2026 (see the note above), so its audit result is not a reason to buy Resemble today.

ElevenLabs splits cloning into two tiers. Instant Voice Cloning works from a short sample and starts on the Starter plan. Professional Voice Cloning needs 30 minutes to 3 hours of clean audio, takes several hours to train, and requires a live verification step — and can only clone your own voice, according to guides checked against the official [ElevenLabs pricing page](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing) in November 2026.

Descript builds consent into the workflow: before AI Speech generates anything, the speaker records a scripted permission statement that stays attached to the project, according to a podcast-tool roundup on [AI Tools Digest](https://www.aitoolsdigest.com/blog/top-ai-voice-cloning-tools-podcasts-2026). Of the tools still on sale, Descript's recorded consent statement and ElevenLabs' live verification step for professional cloning are the strongest consent checks available — Resemble AI matched that standard in the March 2025 audit, with a real-time recording for the first clone plus watermarking, but it no longer sells voice cloning. Speechify's instant cloning comes with a spoken consent check and a watermark, according to a comparison on [Bland](https://www.bland.ai/compare/bland-speech-vs-speechify-voice-cloning).

Two questions the lists never answer: what happens to your clone when you cancel, and who owns the audio you generate? It varies by vendor and by plan, and the terms page is the only authority. Before you pay, check the terms for clone deletion after cancellation, commercial use, and whether the vendor trains on your voice samples. ElevenLabs puts the commercial licence on its Starter plan and up; Speechify's reader Premium plan is personal-use only, according to a Speechify review on [App-Duo](https://app-duo.com/en/apps/speechify).

## What a Month of Cloning Actually Costs

Plan prices say little; what matters is how many finished minutes a plan produces. Every vendor counts usage differently — characters, media hours plus AI credits, hours per year, or words. Convert before you compare.

ElevenLabs charges roughly one credit per character. Its free plan includes 10,000 credits a month, about 10 minutes of audio, with no commercial licence. Paid plans run $6–$990 a month ($5–$825 a month on annual billing): Starter includes 30,000 credits, about 30 minutes; Creator includes 121,000 credits, about two hours, plus professional voice cloning; Pro includes 600,000 credits, about 10 hours. Finished audio costs roughly $0.17–$0.20 per 1,000 characters — about a minute of speech — on the lower tiers, according to the official [ElevenLabs pricing page](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing) in November 2026.

Descript bills on two quotas at once. Hobbyist includes 10 media hours and 400 AI credits a month; Creator includes 30 media hours and 800 AI credits; Business includes 40 media hours and 1,500 AI credits, according to the official [Descript pricing page](https://www.descript.com/pricing). Paid plans run $24–$65 a month ($16–$50 a month on annual billing), according to the official [Descript pricing page](https://www.descript.com/pricing), and cloning is included from the entry paid plan.

Murf works on an annual allowance: its Creator plan includes 24 hours of generation per year — 2 hours a month — and paid plans run $29–$99 a month ($19–$66 a month on annual billing), according to the official [Murf pricing page](https://murf.ai/pricing). Voice cloning itself is not in the standard Creator plan; it is a managed or enterprise add-on.

Speechify splits into three products with separate billing. The reader Premium plan runs $12–$29 a month depending on whether you pay monthly or yearly, and it is personal-use only. Studio, the product that makes publishable voiceovers with cloning, runs $100–$300 a year (Starter to Creator; no monthly price is published), and the developer API runs $10–$499 a month, according to Speechify's official pricing pages.

HeyGen is the exception in this section: it sells video, not audio minutes. Its Creator plan runs $24–$29 a month for 600 video credits, and a finished minute costs 4 credits on Avatar III but 48 on Avatar V — so the same plan buys about 150 minutes of basic avatar video or roughly 12 minutes of Avatar V. Judge it per video minute, not per audio minute, according to [HeyGen's pricing page](https://www.heygen.com/pricing), checked November 2026.

## ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs is the reference point for the whole category. ## Descript AI Speech

Descript is an editor that happens to clone voices, and that is exactly why podcasters pick it. You fix a misread word by editing the transcript text, and Regenerate rebuilds just that word in the host's cloned voice, in place, in the existing waveform. No other tool on this list patches audio that fast.

The October 12, 2026 release moved Descript's AI Speech onto ElevenLabs v4, with clones now sounding closer to the person they were cloned from, according to [GLAD-AI-TOR](https://glad-ia-tor.com/blog/voice-cloning/descript-elevenlabs-v4-voice-clones).

- **Pros:** fastest fix for a flubbed line; recorded consent statement built in; full editor, transcription and Studio Sound included.
- **Cons:** a half-step behind ElevenLabs Professional on long, emotional passages; dual quota empties fast; not a high-volume voice API.
- **Best for:** podcasters and video editors who record first and fix later.

## Murf

Murf is a business voiceover studio with cloning attached, not a cloning tool with a studio attached. Its library of 200+ voices across 35+ languages is tuned for instructional delivery, and the editor is built around scripts and slides rather than single clips.

Voice cloning is a managed or enterprise add-on rather than a self-service feature on the standard plans. Murf integrates with Canva, WordPress, PowerPoint and Google Slides.

- **Pros:** clean narration tuned for training; script-and-slide workflow; integrations with the tools course teams already use.
- **Cons:** cloning gated behind higher or managed tiers; less emotional range than ElevenLabs; annual generation cap punishes daily publishers.
- **Best for:** corporate training, e-learning courses, and internal communications.

## Speechify

Speechify is the fastest way to hear a clone: record a 10–30 second sample, and the cloned voice can then speak in any supported language. Its Simba 3.5 model, launched 21 October 2026, speaks 48 languages under one model, according to a Speechify comparison on [Bland](https://www.bland.ai/compare/bland-speech-vs-speechify-voice-cloning).

The reader app is personal-use only: no commercial distribution of audio. Publishable voiceovers with cloning live in Studio or the developer API. [Litmus Tools](https://litmustools.com/review/speechify/) also documents a pattern of post-cancellation billing complaints on BBB and Reddit.

- **Pros:** fastest cloning from a short sample; 48 languages on one model; spoken consent check and watermark.
- **Cons:** reader plan has no commercial use; Studio and API are billed separately; billing complaints documented by users.
- **Best for:** listening to documents in a familiar voice, and developers who want a cloning API — voice cloning needs a paid API plan, $10–$499 a month.

## How to Choose

Start from the job, not the leaderboard. The quality gap between the top three is smaller than the workflow gap, and the wrong workflow costs more than the wrong voice.

- **You narrate videos, podcasts or audiobooks in your own voice:** ElevenLabs. Cheapest instant cloning, best-sounding professional clones, licence from the lowest paid plan.
- **You record first and fix mistakes later:** Descript AI Speech. Nothing else patches a single word inside a finished episode.
- **You clone voices for clients or a regulated industry:** Descript AI Speech. Its recorded consent statement creates a paper trail. ElevenLabs professional cloning is your own voice only — a client must create and verify their clone in their own ElevenLabs account and share it with you privately, so it is not a way to clone a client's voice yourself.
- **You make training videos for a company:** Murf. The script-and-slide studio matters more than the last 5% of realism.
- **You mostly listen, and sometimes publish:** Speechify, but buy Studio, not the reader app.
- **You need a face on screen speaking another language:** HeyGen, paired with [the best AI image generators](/posts/best-ai-image-generators/) for thumbnails.

## FAQ

**Is AI voice cloning legal?**

Cloning your own voice is legal. Cloning someone else's voice without permission can break platform rules and, in some places, the law — several US states now restrict unauthorised voice replicas.

**How much audio do I need to clone a voice?**

It depends on the tier. ElevenLabs instant cloning works from 1–2 minutes, and Speechify from 10–30 seconds. Professional-grade clones need far more: ElevenLabs professional cloning trains on 30 minutes to 3 hours of clean audio.

**Can I use a cloned voice commercially?**

Only on a plan that includes a commercial licence. ElevenLabs includes one from its Starter plan; Speechify's reader Premium plan is personal-use only, so commercial audio needs Studio or the API.

**What happens to my voice clone if I cancel?**

It varies by vendor, and the terms page is the only authority. Some vendors delete clones when the account closes; others keep them until you ask for deletion.

## A Short Checklist Before You Pay

Record a clean sample in a quiet room. Confirm the plan you buy includes cloning and a commercial licence — free tiers and personal plans usually do not. Count your script's characters and divide by the monthly allowance, then add 20% for retakes. And get written consent from anyone whose voice is not your own, before the first clone is made.
