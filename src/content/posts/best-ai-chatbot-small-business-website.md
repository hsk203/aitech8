## What a small business chatbot actually does

A chatbot does three jobs. It answers repeat questions: hours, service area, delivery times, whether you take bookings, how long a job takes. It captures leads: name, phone, what they need, when. And if you connect it to a calendar, it books.

A chatbot pays when visitors ask the same questions and the bot can do something useful with the answer, such as capture a lead or book a slot.

It tends not to pay when traffic is thin, every enquiry needs a site visit, or you already answer every form within minutes — then a contact form and a fast reply beat any bot. The questions visitors do ask are still useful: our guide to the [best AI copywriting tools for ecommerce](/posts/best-ai-copywriting-tools-ecommerce/) shows how to turn real customer wording into product copy.

Two things the comparison pages rarely spell out matter more than the feature list: what happens when your quota runs out, and how you will know whether the bot is helping. Both are covered below.

## What happens when your AI quota runs out mid-month

Every chatbot here sells you a monthly allowance — conversations, message credits, or resolutions. What the pricing pages bury is what happens when it runs out — and the three possible answers feel very different at 10 pm on a busy Saturday.

**The bot stops.** Some free plans simply stop answering: a visitor types a question, gets nothing, and leaves. This is the worst outcome.

**The bot falls back to a form.** Better tools switch to lead capture: "We're offline — leave your details and we'll reply in the morning." Check that your tool does this before you buy.

**The bill quietly grows.** Tidio's pricing FAQ says you can turn on automatic upgrades at checkout: at 95% of a limit, it moves up to the next tier and you pay that tier's price from then on ([tidio.com/pricing](https://www.tidio.com/pricing/)). Chatbase offers auto-recharge credits that top up your balance when it falls below a threshold you set ([chatbase.co/pricing](https://www.chatbase.co/pricing)). Both are useful, and both can surprise you if you never set a cap.

Before launch, count your current monthly enquiries and buy a quota at least 50% above that number. Decide in advance whether auto-upgrade is on or off, and set a spending alert if it is on. The comparison table above lists what each tool does when its allowance runs out.

## How to tell if the bot is actually helping

Vendors love to quote resolution rates — "resolves 70% of chats" — but those numbers come from each vendor's own customers and cannot be checked. Ignore them. Measure your own bot with three numbers you can read yourself.

**How many chats ended without a human.** Open the conversation log once a week and count the chats the bot closed on its own. Under half after a month means the bot is undertrained — usually because your site has too little content for it to learn from.

**How many chats turned into a lead.** A bot that answers questions but captures no names or numbers is a FAQ page with extra steps. Count the leads per week.

**What the bot got wrong.** Read the ten longest chats every week. Wrong answers cluster around the same gaps: a price that changed, a service you stopped offering, a policy page that was never written. Fix the source page, not the bot. Keep a list of the questions it could not answer — that is your next month of website content, and our guide to the [SEO blog workflow with Semrush and Grammarly](/posts/seo-blog-workflow-semrush-grammarly/) shows how to turn them into articles.

One warning: a chatbot that invents an answer can cost you money. In Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024 BCCRT 149), a Canadian tribunal held Air Canada responsible after its website chatbot promised a bereavement fare that was not the airline's policy ([the decision](https://www.canlii.org/en/bc/bccrt/doc/2024/2024bccrt149/2024bccrt149.html)). Keep the bot's answers tied to pages you control.

## The chatbots

### Tidio

Tidio combines live chat, ticketing, and Lyro, its AI agent, in one inbox ([tidio.com/pricing](https://www.tidio.com/pricing/)). The free plan covers 50 human conversations a month plus a one-time quota of 50 Lyro AI conversations.

What makes it stand out is the real inbox: a person can take over any chat with full history. The catch is the billing unit: Lyro's 50 free conversations are a one-time allotment, not monthly ([tidio.com/pricing](https://www.tidio.com/pricing/)).

**Pricing** (checked November 24, 2026): Starter $32.50–$33 a month for 50 conversations; Growth from $62.50–$63 a month (100 conversations to start). 7-day free trial, no card needed.

### Chatbase

Chatbase is a well-known AI agent builder: train an agent on your website content and uploaded files, then embed it on your site ([chatbase.co/pricing](https://www.chatbase.co/pricing/)). Customer data is not used to train models, and the vendor lists GDPR and SOC 2 Type II compliance.

Its strength is maturity: actions, integrations, and a helpdesk included — and no seat fees. The trade-off is the price: Basic is $120–$150 a month for 4,000 message credits, and actions can consume extra credits — a booking question can cost twice the credits of a simple answer, according to Chatbase's own FAQ.

**Pricing** (checked November 24, 2026): Basic $120–$150 a month (4,000 credits); Business $400–$500 (15,000); 7-day trial. Add-ons: auto-recharge credits, no-branding, extra agents and storage.

### HubSpot

HubSpot's chatbot builder lives inside its free CRM, and chats attach to contact records automatically, so sales sees the history next to the deal ([hubspot.com](https://www.hubspot.com/crm/am_chatbot)).

The appeal is the price: the basic chatbot is free for up to two users ([hubspot.com](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/service)), and lead qualification, meeting booking, and ticket flows are included. The limitation is that the free builder is rule-based, not AI.

Real AI answers need Service Hub Professional, and its Customer Agent costs a fixed 50 HubSpot Credits per resolved conversation (50 cents at HubSpot's credit rate) ([hubspot.com](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/hubspots-customer-agent-and-prospecting-agent-now-you-pay-when-the-task-is-complete)). If booked meetings pile up, our [best AI meeting notetakers](/posts/best-ai-meeting-notetakers/) roundup covers tools that handle the notes once the bot has booked them.

**Pricing** (checked November 24, 2026): free plan; Starter seats $7–$20 per seat a month depending on billing; Professional from $90–$100. Check the official site — HubSpot reprices often.

### Crisp

Crisp puts live chat, email, and messaging into one shared inbox, with a built-in CRM and knowledge base, priced flat per workspace with no limit on conversations ([crisp.chat](https://crisp.chat/en/pricing/)).

Where it wins is the flat price: add teammates without the bill moving. The free plan covers 2 seats, the chat widget, and a shared inbox; Hugo AI is included on paid plans.

The downside is what the free plan leaves out: no chatbot, triggers, or knowledge base — AI starts on the paid Mini plan, and the full chatbot builder needs the Essentials plan.

**Pricing** (checked November 25, 2026): free plan; paid plans run $45–$295 a month per workspace (Mini to Plus), with a 14-day free trial.

### Intercom Fin

Fin is Intercom's AI agent, working inside Intercom or on top of another helpdesk, and you pay per "outcome" — a resolved conversation — rather than per message ([intercom.com](https://intercom.com/pricing)).

Paying for results, not attempts, is the appeal: an unanswered chat costs nothing, and a full helpdesk sits around it. The risk is that the bill grows with success: at $0.99–$1.99 per outcome, 1,000 resolved chats a month costs about $990–$1,990 in Fin fees alone, before seats. A minimum commitment applies — 50 outcomes a month on standalone Fin.

**Pricing** (checked November 24, 2026): $0.99–$1.99 per Fin outcome (text vs. Fin Voice; a sales qualification costs just under ten dollars). A configured Procedure handoff counts as an outcome; a plain handoff is free. Seats: Essential $19–$29, Advanced and Expert $85–$132 a seat a month (annual billing). No seat fee on another helpdesk. Check the official site — Intercom reprices often.

### Botsonic

Botsonic is Writesonic's chatbot builder, with the lowest entry price here, and it can hand chats to Zendesk or Freshdesk agents ([botsonic.com](https://botsonic.com/pricing)).

The draw is the price: $16–$19 a month for 1,000 messages, with a 7-day free trial. Watch the add-ons, though: removing branding costs about three times the entry plan, and the Zendesk handoff add-on costs more than ten times it.

**Pricing** (checked November 25, 2026): Starter $16–$19 a month (1,000 messages); Professional $41–$49 a month (3,000 messages); Advanced $249–$299 a month (12,000 messages).

## How to choose

**Do you already live in a CRM or helpdesk?** If your team runs on HubSpot, use its free chatbot builder first — it costs nothing. On Zendesk or Freshdesk, Botsonic's handoff is worth a look.

**Will a person answer chats, or should the bot work alone?** If someone will take over chats during the day, pick a tool with a real inbox: Tidio or Crisp. If nobody will staff chat, pick an AI-first builder: Chatbase (AI Q&A and actions, with a lightweight helpdesk included) or Botsonic. Tidio and Crisp are live-chat-first inboxes; Chatbase is AI-first. A bot with no human behind it still needs a fallback — a lead form, not silence.

**How many conversations will you actually get?** Count last month's emails and form fills, add 50%, and buy that quota. Under 35 enquiries a month (50 with the buffer), Tidio Starter covers you; between 50 and 100 conversations, step up to Growth. Over 500, compare Chatbase's credit math against Intercom Fin's per-outcome price with your real numbers. Remember: credits and conversations are not the same unit, so read the FAQ before you compare prices.

## FAQ

**How much does an AI chatbot cost a small business?**

Most small sites pay $16–$63 a month for ongoing AI answers. Free plans exist on Tidio, HubSpot, and Crisp, but none of them covers recurring AI chat: HubSpot's free builder is rules-only, Tidio's free plan gives 50 one-time Lyro AI conversations, and Crisp's free plan has no AI at all. The expensive mistakes are the billing model, not the monthly fee — per-outcome pricing at high volume, or auto-upgrade tiers you never capped.

**Will a chatbot replace my customer service?**

No. A chatbot answers the same ten questions at 10 pm and hands the rest to a person. Gartner found that only 27% of customers would try a chatbot again after a negative experience ([gartner.com](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-02-gartner-finds-only-27-percent-of-customers-would-try-a-chatbot-again-after-a-negative-experience)). A bot that hands off gracefully protects that trust; one that traps visitors in a loop destroys it.

**What about privacy and the EU AI Act?**

Tell visitors they are talking to an AI in the bot's first message — the EU AI Act's transparency rules apply from August 2, 2026 to any site with EU visitors ([artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). This is general information, not legal advice. Also check where each vendor stores chat data: Chatbase and Tidio both publish GDPR compliance statements.

## Getting started this week

Pick the tool that matches your three answers above, then give yourself one week.

- **Day 1:** install the widget and feed it your five most-asked questions.
- **Day 2:** write the fallback message for when the bot does not know, and the handoff message for when a human takes over.
- **Days 3–7:** read every conversation each morning, fix the source page behind each wrong answer, and add the questions it could not answer to your content list.

The best-answered questions make good short videos — see our [best AI video generator for YouTube Shorts](/posts/best-ai-video-generator-for-youtube-shorts/).

The bot will not be perfect in week one — it improves as you fix the source pages it reads and add the questions it missed. Start with Tidio if you want live chat and AI together, Chatbase if you want an AI-first bot that answers and acts from your content, and HubSpot's free builder if you already live in HubSpot.
