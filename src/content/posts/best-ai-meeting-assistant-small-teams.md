## What an AI meeting assistant actually does

Split the meeting into four jobs. ClickUp's roundup makes the same point: the value is what happens to the transcript next.

**Before:** agenda building and a briefing on what was agreed last time. Fellow is built around this; Notion AI leans on it too.

**During:** live transcription and live answers. Otter leads with a real-time transcript; Read AI adds speaker coaching.

**After:** a summary, action items with owners and deadlines, and a searchable record. Fireflies and MeetGeek push action items into task tools and CRMs, not an unread email.

**The handoff:** the step most teams skip. The assistant should file the outcome where the work lives — a CRM for a sales call, a task tool for a project meeting, Slack for a standup. Notes also make good content raw material: our guide to the [SEO blog workflow with Semrush and Grammarly](/posts/seo-blog-workflow-semrush-grammarly/) shows how a transcript becomes a draft, and the [best AI copywriting tools for ecommerce](/posts/best-ai-copywriting-tools-ecommerce/) covers turning notes into customer copy.

## MeetGeek: the best all-rounder for a small team

MeetGeek is Fit Small Business's top pick for small businesses: recording, transcription in more than 100 languages, automated summaries, highlights and integrations at a small-team price. Its free Basic plan gives 3 hours of transcription a month, a 2-hour cap per recording, unlimited AI summaries, and — unusually — API and MCP access, according to MeetGeek's own pricing page.

Entry paid plans run $10–$16 per user per month depending on billing cycle. HappyScribe's roundup of the best AI meeting note takers agrees on the shape of the free plan — 3 hours of transcription a month, with transcripts stored for three months — and on the meeting-type-aware summaries. Pro adds 20 transcription hours a month, Zapier integrations, and AI workflows that push summaries into Slack, your CRM or your docs. Business, in the $17–$27 per user per month range, adds unlimited transcription, HD video recording, 12-month video storage, team spaces, and custom branding. Bot-free recording is on every plan, including the free Basic tier, via the desktop app or browser extension. Note the free plan has no downloads or exports, and workflow integrations such as Zapier sit on paid plans — though API and MCP access is included even on Basic — and transcripts are kept for three months.

## Fathom: the free plan that is hard to beat

Fathom's free plan includes unlimited recordings and transcripts with no seat minimum — a point Panstag's comparison makes central and TeamShift's November 2026 recheck confirms. Entry paid plans sit in the $15–$20 per user per month range, adding AI action items and a conversational assistant for questioning a call. CRM field sync needs the Business plan, at $25–$34 per user per month.

Its assistant angle is the "Ask Fathom" chat: after a call, ask what was decided, who owns what, or what a client said about the price, instead of re-reading a transcript. Basic AI summaries are unlimited on the free plan, but advanced summaries, custom templates, Ask Fathom and auto action items are limited — Panstag notes five AI-summarized calls a month — so a team that meets daily hits the ceiling fast. HappyScribe's roundup of the best AI meeting note takers describes the same ceiling — advanced features on the first five calls a month, then basic summaries. Bot-free capture is in beta on Mac only. Superhuman acquired Fathom in September 2026; Fathom says nothing changes for users — same app, same login, same free plan. An acquisition can still mean pricing or plan changes later, so re-check the free plan's limits before you build a workflow on it.

## Fireflies: the sales team's assistant

Fireflies is the pick when meetings feed a pipeline. Fireflies' own pricing page lists a free plan with unlimited transcription, 400 minutes of storage and limited AI summaries; entry paid plans run $10–$18 per seat per month. It joins Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Webex and more, transcribes in more than 100 languages, and syncs notes into HubSpot and Salesforce. Automatic field sync (Autofill CRM) needs the Business plan, at $19–$29 per seat per month.

ClickUp's roundup highlights Fireflies' AskFred AI chat, which answers questions across your whole meeting archive. Paid plans add AI scorecards that grade sales calls — useful for a small sales team coaching itself, overkill for an internal standup. If your meetings are mostly with clients, Fireflies pays for itself in CRM hygiene. SpotSaaS's roundup of the best AI meeting assistants for teams names Fireflies its best overall pick on the same grounds — wide language coverage, a searchable archive, and storage limits lifted on the Business plan. That is also why Fireflies stays the sales pick even though it joins as a bot: when every client call has to land in the CRM, the handoff outweighs the bot concern (see "Bot or bot-free" below).

## tl;dv: hiring, HR, and calls in many languages

tl;dv is Fit Small Business's pick for HR teams and hiring managers, thanks to chaptered recordings, timestamped highlight clips, and cross-meeting search, so a panel can compare candidates without re-watching whole calls. ClickUp's roundup of the best AI meeting assistants for small business lists the same strengths — chaptered recordings, highlight clips, cross-meeting insights and 30+ languages. It supports more than 30 languages; tl;dv's own pricing page lists paid plans at $18–$29 per seat per month on annual billing ($29–$39 month-to-month), with Business at the top end.

The free plan handles unlimited recordings; paid tiers unlock unlimited AI summaries and CRM sync. A team that does not run interviews or multilingual sales calls will not get its money's worth.

## Otter: live help while the meeting is happening

Otter is the oldest name here and still the best at the live job: a real-time transcript, slide capture, and an AI chat you can query mid-meeting. Otter's own pricing page lists the plans: its free Basic plan gives 300 transcription minutes a month with a 30-minute cap per conversation, and entry paid plans run $8–$17 per user per month — the cheapest annual entry for live transcription with a bot in the call, according to TeamShift's recheck. Business runs $19.99–$30 per user per month.

The 30-minute free cap is the catch: an hour-long meeting stops transcribing halfway through unless you pay. SpotSaaS's roundup of the best AI meeting assistants for teams calls Otter the value pick for English-speaking teams, confirming the live transcription and the free plan's minute cap. Otter also works with uploaded audio files.

## Granola: the assistant for teams that refuse bots

Granola runs as a desktop app, captures audio from your own device, and never sends a bot into the call. TeamShift's comparison names it the pick for anyone who does not want a bot in the meeting — a visible bot changes how clients speak. Granola also enriches the notes you type yourself with AI, rather than replacing them. Knowledgework's Granola AI review, built from Granola's own pricing, security and help pages, reaches the same verdict — notes steered by your own typed lines, no bot in the call, and the 30-day history limit on the free plan.

According to Granola's own pricing page, the free Basic plan covers unlimited meetings but only shows the last 30 days of notes, and paid plans run $14–$35 per user per month, from Business to Enterprise. Granola works on Mac and Windows for in-person meetings too, with consent. The trade-off is coverage: it is a personal notes tool first, so a team needing a shared archive with CRM sync will outgrow it.

## Bot or bot-free: the decision most teams skip

Most assistants join as a visible participant — a bot with a name, announced to everyone. That is transparent, and in some places the legally safer default, since recording laws vary. But it changes the room: clients speak more carefully, and some will ask you to turn it off.

Bot-free capture records from your own device or browser instead. Granola and Krisp work only this way; Fathom offers it in beta on Mac, and MeetGeek, tl;dv and Fellow through a desktop app or browser extension.

The practical rule: if your meetings are mostly internal, a bot is fine and cheaper. If they are with clients, candidates or partners, bot-free capture is worth paying for. Sales calls are the exception: when every client call has to land in the CRM, the handoff outweighs the bot concern, so teams that need that accept the bot — see the Fireflies section above. Either way, tell people the meeting is being recorded and why.

## What to watch out for

**Free plans are not the same shape.** Fathom caps summaries, Otter caps minutes, Fireflies caps storage, MeetGeek caps hours. Read the cap that matches your meeting volume.

**Summaries are not minutes.** AI summaries catch action items and decisions well and miss nuance and tone. If a meeting decides something important, read the transcript, not just the summary, and confirm it in writing.

**Action items need owners.** An action item without a named owner and a date is a wish. When you trial a tool, check whether action items reach your task list with owners and deadlines, or whether someone retypes them.

**Privacy and consent are yours, not the tool's.** Check where recordings are stored, how long they are kept — MeetGeek publishes retention per plan — and whether the vendor trains models on your meetings.

**Prices move.** Every price here is a range from vendor pages and November 2026 roundups. Vendors change prices and free-plan limits without announcement — Sembly's free plan itself came and went during 2026: ToolCheckHQ's November 2026 recheck notes older coverage saying it was removed in March 2026, while Sembly's own pricing page lists a Free plan with 5 meeting hours again.

## FAQ

**Do I need an assistant if I already have a notetaker?**

Only if you want help beyond the transcript. A notetaker records and summarizes; an assistant also handles agendas, live questions, action items, and the CRM handoff.

**Will clients mind a bot in the call?**

Some will, and a few will ask you to turn it off. Say at the start of the call that it is being recorded and why. If most of your meetings are with clients, pick a bot-free tool such as Granola or Krisp. The exception is sales calls that must land in the CRM — there the handoff outweighs the bot concern; see the Fireflies section.

**What is the best free meeting assistant for a small team?**

Fathom: its free plan includes unlimited recordings and transcripts with no seat minimum. Advanced AI summaries are capped at a few calls a month, so heavy users still need a paid plan.

**Can it send action items to my CRM?**

Yes — that is the main difference from a plain notetaker. Fireflies syncs fields into HubSpot and Salesforce on its Business plan, MeetGeek pushes summaries into CRMs and Slack, and tl;dv syncs with CRMs on paid plans. Check your CRM is on the integration list first.

## The bottom line

Start with Fathom for the strongest free plan, or MeetGeek for the most complete assistant at a small-team price. Move to Fireflies when meetings feed a CRM, to tl;dv when you hire or sell in several languages, and to Granola when a bot is a deal-breaker. The wrong choice is paying before you have hit a free-tier limit — where a free tier exists.
